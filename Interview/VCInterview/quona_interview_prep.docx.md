## **4.1 Core Thesis Statement**

Below is a framework to synthesize your points into a seamless, high-conviction pitch:

---

### **The Conversational Pitch**

**The Setup: The Macro Context**

"My thesis is rooted in a simple operational reality: the marginal cost of building fintech software is trending toward zero. With AI and modern infrastructure, the technical barrier to launching an app has effectively vanished. **The Closer: Aligning with Quona's B2B Thesis**

Securing ownership in these infrastructure plays early—as Quona is doing now—is the only way to protect target returns before these moats trigger massive valuation reratings."

Analyzing broader market dynamics alongside Quona’s recent velocity reveals a definitive pivot toward B2B infrastructure, marked by leaner median check sizes and expanding valuation multiples. This trend reinforces the "infrastructure" thesis: in emerging markets, B2C unit economics are structurally constrained by unsustainable CAC. B2B models provide the necessary rails to solve distribution friction. While infrastructure-layer plays command a valuation premium due to their sticky revenue moats, securing earlier, smaller entry points is the optimal lever for protecting target ownership in an increasingly competitive environment.

# **6\. Valuation Framework**

For early-stage fintech in emerging markets, pure DCF is rarely the right tool. Show you understand when to use which lens — and that valuation is ultimately a negotiation anchored in what the next investor will pay.

## **5.1 Method 1: Revenue Multiples (Most Common at Series A/B)**

For a Quona-stage company: 5–15x ARR is typical for high-growth emerging market B2B fintech.

| Example A B2B lending infrastructure company at $4M ARR, growing 2.5x YoY, with 80% gross margins — I'd anchor at 8–12x ARR, or $32–48M post-money, depending on NRR, market size, and team quality. |
| :---- |

## **5.2 Method 2: Unit Economics (LTV/CAC)**

* What does a customer cost to acquire (CAC)? What is their lifetime value (LTV)?

* Is LTV/CAC \> 3x? Is payback \< 18 months?

* Quona's Contabilizei case: embedded finance users had 30–50% higher LTV — this is the kind of metric that re-rates a valuation

* For lending businesses: also assess net interest margin (NIM), credit loss rates, and cost of funds

## **5.3 Method 3: Comparable Transactions**

* Quona's own exits as comps: Fisdom at $150M, IndiaMart IPO, Coins.ph acquisition

* For live companies: Nubank, PagSeguro comparable growth-stage multiples

* For lending businesses: Price/Book of listed EM fintechs (Spandana, Five Star Finance: 2–4x book)

## **5.4 The VC Ownership Math (Shows You Understand the Model)**

| The Fund Math Lens At Quona's target of 15–20% ownership with a $2–6M check, the implied post-money valuation range is $10–40M. For that ownership to generate meaningful fund-level returns — say 5x on a $332M fund needing $50M+ gross returns per winner — the company needs to exit at $200–500M+. So the core question is: can this company realistically reach $500M in enterprise value in 5–8 years given the market size and growth rate? |

**THE CORE INSIGHT**

Jai Kisan embeds credit into the dealer networks that serve India's 120 million smallholder farmers. **WHY IT FITS QUONA'S THESIS**

* Serves the financially excluded: 70% of India's farmers lack access to formal credit — Jai Kisan's exact target market

* B2B revenue model: works with dealer networks, not direct-to-consumer — matches Quona's current B2B tilt

**WHAT TO SAY IN YOUR INTERVIEW**

"The company I'd bring to Quona is Jai Kisan — a rural fintech in India that's cracked the distribution problem that's stopped every bank from serving farmers at scale. Those dealers have 10-year relationships with farmers and real-time transaction data — that's the underwriting signal. Jai Kisan's Bharat Khata app sits in the middle of this commerce flow, processing billions in rural transactions, and they've now acquired an NBFC licence so they can put that data to work on their own balance sheet. At \~$180M valuation and \~$6M ARR, you're getting a company with a defensible data moat, 70+ investors signaling conviction, and a product that directly addresses India's $200B+ formal agri credit gap. **RISKS TO ADDRESS PROACTIVELY**

* Still loss-making: ₹51Cr loss on ₹47.9Cr revenue in FY24. Address the path to unit economics — the NBFC licence reduces cost of capital and on-book loans should improve NIM

* Regulatory risk: RBI can change priority sector lending rules or NBFC capital requirements

* Climate exposure: agri lending is inherently weather-sensitive — push on how Jai Kisan underwrites drought and flood risk

## **6.2 Twinco Capital — Portfolio Company Deep Dive**

| Type QUONA PORTFOLIO — defend as an existing investment | Key details Supply chain finance · Amsterdam/Madrid · Founded 2019 · $73.3M raised · Quona led $12M (Jan 2023\) |

**THE CORE INSIGHT**

Twinco closes the $2.5 trillion global trade finance gap by embedding purchase-order financing into the supply chains of large multinational retailers. Their model: engage a major buyer, then fund that company's suppliers in emerging markets — Bangladesh, Vietnam, Indonesia, Pakistan, Turkey — advancing up to 60% of purchase order value upfront with the remainder paid on delivery. Suppliers get liquidity exactly when they need it most (production, not delivery). Buyers get sustainable, ESG-compliant supply chains.

**WHY QUONA BACKED IT — WHAT TO REFERENCE**

* Directly addresses the $2.5T trade finance gap that 'disproportionately affects SMEs in emerging countries' — Monica Brand Engel's exact words at the deal announcement

* B2B model with Fortune 500 corporate anchors: counterparty credit risk is a Walmart or H\&M, not a Bangladeshi factory

* ESG embedded in product design: the 'Twinco ESG Tilt' incentivizes suppliers to improve environmental and social practices through preferential pricing — a real impact mechanism, not marketing

* Data moat: Twinco continuously collects commercial, financial, and ESG performance data on thousands of manufacturers across 13 countries — becoming a supply chain intelligence platform

* Female founder team with deep domain expertise: Sandra Nolasco (ex-BBVA, 20+ years in structured trade finance) and Carmen Marin (ex-Banco Santander) — Quona explicitly cited team strength

**WHAT TO SAY IN YOUR INTERVIEW**

"Twinco is a company that exemplifies what Quona does better than any other fund could. The core insight — using a large buyer's purchase order as the collateral to fund their emerging-market suppliers — solves a problem sitting in the $2.5T trade finance gap for decades. Banks won't touch purchase order funding because it's pre-delivery risk. Twinco built the model that makes it work, using commercial relationship data as underwriting signal. What makes this more than just a lending business is the ESG intelligence layer: Twinco's data platform now collects sustainability metrics on manufacturers in Bangladesh, Vietnam, and Pakistan that large corporates literally cannot get anywhere else. That's where the pricing power and long-term defensibility come from. Quona led the $12M round in January 2023, and by October of that year Twinco had secured a €50M debt facility from BBVA Spark — a signal of product-market fit that de-risks the next equity round. Exit path: acquired by a major trade finance bank (Standard Chartered, HSBC, BBVA) or becomes a supply chain data platform commanding a software multiple."

**RISKS TO ADDRESS PROACTIVELY**

* Buyer concentration: if a major retailer exits a sourcing country, Twinco's volume in that country drops — push on buyer diversification

* Pre-delivery risk: Twinco funds up to delivery — need to understand what happens if a supplier defaults or can't deliver

* Competitive moat: as the model matures, large banks may replicate it — Twinco's defensibility relies on data and ESG capabilities staying ahead of bank products
